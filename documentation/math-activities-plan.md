## 1. What This Feature Does

Proposal: documentation/math-activities-proposal.html (plan-redline publication 1, 2026-09-29; a standalone offline view of this plan, which remains the source of truth)

Plan 02 widens what a parent can ask Dry Math for and adds one distinct worksheet family, without raising any quantity, ten-frame, Two Whats and a Wow or Count, Compare & Make ceiling (all stay at 20). On top of plan 01's worksheet-first panel it adds:

- **Carrying and borrowing as an explicit Dry Math choice (P6):** a page on which every addition carries and every subtraction borrows at least once, within the chosen range, never negative, and never inferred from the dormant v1 `allowRegrouping` flag.
- **Multiplication facts and exact division by fact family (P3):** parent-selected families 0–12, factors 0–12, dividends through 144, 3 × 4 and 4 × 3 distinct, division always exact with a nonzero divisor, and one stated zero-dividend rule.
- **Number Bonds / Missing Numbers (P5):** missing number sentences and number bond pictures within 5, 10 or 20, in which every problem has exactly one blank, one intended answer, every number at least 1, and a drawn writing space. Problems that need carrying or borrowing, including every one that makes or crosses ten, appear only when the parent includes them (D-bonds-ten).

It is built because the operator asked for multiplication, division, more representations and a distinct new activity, and because the second UAT review recorded these as the next learning tasks rather than bigger numbers (`documentation/uat-round-2-review.md`, feature backlog). The plan is **evidence first**: Step 21 lands an independent, calibrated arithmetic oracle that already judges the shipped generators and a scripted freeze of every legacy selection's content and request; every later slice must satisfy the oracle, the capacity sweeps and the extended print matrix. Because persisted choices widen, the config moves to `schemaVersion: 3` through plan 01's version chain, additively: every v2 path keeps its meaning, the first explicit save keeps a byte-identical backup, newer versions stay blocked, and every earlier selection keeps byte-identical content at generator version 1.

## 6. Design Decisions

**DD1. Evidence first, from an oracle that cannot share production's mistakes (D9, D49).** The oracle lands in Step 21 and must already agree with the shipped generators before any content changes; each slice then extends it with its own rule and is DONE only when its generator satisfies it. Independence is by method (string columns with propagation instead of modulo arithmetic; brute force and closed forms instead of generator enumeration; exhaustive solving instead of formula answers) and by import: a TypeScript-AST scan fails on any value import from `src/`, calibrated with a synthetic one. Calibration follows measurement validity: every Appendix B.4 anchor was computed while writing this plan by an independent script and seven were cross-checked against the shipped enumerator; the oracle must reproduce each anchor, a known-good document must score zero violations and each known-garbage document exactly its named code (Appendix B.5) before any verdict is trusted. It judges production artifacts end to end: documents from the real projection and registry, and in the browser the rendered DOM text of worksheet and key. Union-coverage seed ranges are sized, never guessed (D49): a cell with N candidates and a page budget b needs a seed count s with N × (1 − b/N)^s below 1e-4, so 400 seeds cover every cell of at most 400 candidates at the Long standard budget of 18, the 760-candidate both-operations sentence cell uses 1,000, the 190-candidate pictures cell at a budget of 10 uses 800, and larger cells are proved by membership and targeted presence instead of a union. Rejected: recomputing answers with production helpers, which would share any rule defect.

**DD2. Legacy content and requests are frozen by script, not by trust (D10, D42, D53, D-scripted-evidence).** A capture computed after a refactor can encode the bug (project memory), so Step 21 runs `capture-arithmetic-golden.mjs` at its step base (plan 01's pushed commit), commits the output with the full ref inside it, and every later gate re-runs `--compare`. The catalog grid (Appendix C.3) covers every plan 01 catalog selection of every family at three lengths, two scales and seeds 1–8; each case records the SHA-256 of `canonicalContentKey` and of the canonical JSON of the projected request with `generatorVersion` removed, so request continuity is proved, not only item continuity. Catalog cases alone would leave the selections real files hold unproved: plan 01's earlier-settings seeding (plan 01 DD9, Appendix B.3) puts off-catalog Dry Math, Wow and Count, Compare & Make values into saved defaults, shown as "Earlier setting" options (plan 01 DD11). The same capture therefore records a fixed-seed sample of 256 schema-valid v2 selections, off-catalog values included, with each request hash, and Step 23 compares its projection of every upgraded sample selection against those bytes rather than against a projector that Step 23 itself rewrites. Step 22, the v2-freeze step, extends the same script with the older-build verdict and its mirror case, captured at the ref recorded in the keys file (so still plan 01's pushed build) into its own file, `older-build-verdicts.json` (D63): the base ref's classifier and store return `current`, GET 200 and unchanged bytes for a valid v2 control, and `future` with `CONFIG_VERSION_UNSUPPORTED` and unchanged bytes on GET and on a recovery PUT for synthetic `schemaVersion` 3 and 4 configs (MU7), all on files under the capture's own temporary directory. `arithmetic-legacy-golden.test.ts` (in `tests/integration`, because only it can read and hash the committed files, §2) reproduces every hash inside `check`; a perturbation must change a hash. Plan 01's Practice grid and its `--compare` keep running; plan 02's capture lives in `tests/fixtures/math-golden/` because plan 01 freezes `tests/fixtures/golden/`.

**DD3. One owner for the regrouping rule (D11).** Three copies exist (§2). Step 21 moves them into `arithmetic.ts` with identical digit-wise semantics before regrouping becomes a choice, because Step 24 changes how the rule is applied and a divergent copy would silently disagree between generator and validator. Proof: exhaustive equality with the oracle over `0..100` squared for addition and over every pair with left operand at least right operand for subtraction (Appendix B.1 defines no other); for every pair with left operand below right operand, `subtractionBorrows` returns true, exactly as the shipped digit-wise predicate does (the highest column in which the two differ always holds the smaller minuend digit), because Wow's validator applies the rule to false equation choices with no left-at-least-right precondition (`find-the-wow/generator.ts` :462) and so keeps rejecting such a choice; both legacy grids, and an AST scan that allows the regrouping functions and any modulo-by-10 expression only in `arithmetic.ts` and finds no binding of any kind named after a retired name, because Step 21 replaces the two local `regroupingFree` constants with `regroups(…)` calls (test files, including `dry-math.spec.ts`'s independent helpers, are out of scope).

**DD4. Generator versions, requests and normalization (D12, D13, D47).** The rule, written into `CONTRIBUTING.md` in Step 21 and pinned by `generator-versions.test.ts`: (1) a family's `generatorVersion` increments when any request its current version accepts would produce different items for the same seed (enumeration order, filters, shuffle use, capacity, tie-breaking, answers or item fields), and the old implementation stays registered while any stored document relies on it; (2) accepting a request the current version refused, through a new optional request member whose absence keeps every earlier request byte-identical, does not increment; (3) a projector change that maps an existing saved selection to a different request is not a generator-version question: it changes what saved choices mean and needs a config `schemaVersion` bump with a migration; (4) renderer, print-token and key-layout changes do not increment; (5) a new family starts at 1. Rule 2's premise must be true, not assumed. At the base no generator or validator rejects an unknown request member (§2, A45), so a request carrying `practice` would be accepted with the member ignored, and under rule 1 giving the member meaning would force a Dry Math bump. Step 21 therefore makes `validateWorksheetInvariants` refuse any request carrying a `practice` member, for every family, before any slice gives the member meaning (D47); it changes no content because no shipped projector emits the member, and from Step 21 on rule 2 holds literally. Step 24 narrows that refusal to the kind each family accepts. The builds before Step 21, plan 01's pushed build included, accepted and ignored the member; that is harmless only because no projector emits it and no request or document is persisted before plan 03, and `CONTRIBUTING.md` says so. Applied: regrouping and facts are domain extensions under rule 2, so Dry Math stays at 1, proved by both legacy captures, the unchanged known vectors and the no-`practice` property; Wow's refactor changes no item; Number Bonds starts at 1. Rule 1 needs something to compare against for the new kinds as well, because Step 25 edits the Dry Math generator after Step 24 built its `required` path and Step 27 edits the Number Bonds generator after Step 26 built sentences: each content slice therefore commits known vectors for its own kind (D58), every later gate reproduces them unchanged, and `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s rule says a changed vector requires a `generatorVersion` bump. Rule 2's premise also holds inside the plan: until Step 27 the Number Bonds guard refuses a request whose Number Bonds kind has `variant: "bond"` (D61), so accepting pictures in Step 27 changes no request that Number Bonds 1 accepted. `GenerationRequestV1` keeps `schemaVersion: 1` and gains one optional member, `practice?: PracticeRequestV1` (Appendix A.3), absent from every request plan 01 can produce; "Without carrying or borrowing" normalizes to no `practice` at all; every other family's validator refuses it. No request is persisted before plan 03, which persists this final shape. Rejected: `GenerationRequestV2` (churn with no content difference); reading `allowRegrouping` as "require" (it is the dormant v1 flag P6 forbids reading as consent); facts in `capabilities.mathSkills.operations` (the frozen v1 `MathOperation` shared with Wow); bumping Dry Math to 2 (every older request would stop reproducing); rewording rule 2 instead of adding the guard (the premise would stay unenforced).

### B. Content rules and evidence anchors

**B.1 Regrouping (Step 24).** `a + b` regroups when some decimal column's digits sum to 10 or more; `a − b` with `a ≥ b` regroups when some column's minuend digit is smaller than its subtrahend digit. This is exactly today's digit-wise predicate. Subtraction regrouping is defined only for `a ≥ b`, so every oracle equality over subtraction pairs (Step 21 Done when 3) ranges over those pairs only. Under `required` every item regroups; under `without` none does. Operands lie in `0..operandMax`, results in `0..resultMax`, both at most 100.

**B.2 Facts (Step 25).** Multiplication `(a, b)` with `a, b` in `0..12`, answer `a × b`, in family `f` when `a = f` or `b = f`. Division `(dividend, divisor)` with divisor in `1..12`, quotient `q` in `0..12`, dividend = divisor × `q`, answer `q`, in family `f` when divisor = `f` or `q = f`. Key `operation:left:right`, so `multiplication:3:4` and `multiplication:4:3` differ. Canonical order: operations in `FACT_OPERATIONS` order; multiplication by `a`, then `b`; division by divisor, then quotient; the seeded shuffle then slices the budget. Closed forms for a family set `F`: multiplication 169 − (13 − |F|)²; division 156 − (12 − |F without 0|) × (13 − |F|); both operations the sum.

**B.3 Number Bonds (Steps 26, 27).** Wholes run `2..wholeMax` and every part is at least 1. Sentences: addition `left + right = result` with result in `2..wholeMax`, left in `1..result − 1`; subtraction `left − right = result` with left in `2..wholeMax`, right in `1..left − 1`; `missing` is `left` or `right` (for addition an addend; for subtraction the minuend, which is the whole, or the subtrahend); the result is always shown. Sentence identity is form, operation, every displayed number and the blank position, so `? + 7 = 15` and `7 + ? = 15` are distinct, as 3 + 4 and 4 + 3 already are in shipped Dry Math and as P3 rules for 3 × 4 and 4 × 3 (D57); the redline tweak of D57, identity (known addend, result) for addition sentences, would halve the addition counts to `wholeMax × (wholeMax − 1) / 2` and leave subtraction unchanged. Pictures: whole `w` in `2..wholeMax` and shown part `s` in `1..w − 1`, the missing part being `w − s`; identity is `(w, s)`, one task per whole and shown part (D48), and the blank side (`missing` left or right, the shown part on the other side, so `leftPart` and `rightPart` follow) is drawn from the seeded stream after the slice, deterministic for a request and seed and never a second problem. The answer is the missing number. Canonical order, as nested loops from outermost to innermost: sentences loop over the operation in `MATH_OPERATIONS` order; then, for addition, over `result` ascending, then `leftOperand` ascending in `1..result − 1` (so `rightOperand` is `result − leftOperand`), and for subtraction over `leftOperand` (the whole) ascending, then `rightOperand` ascending in `1..leftOperand − 1` (so `result` is `leftOperand − rightOperand`); then over `missing`, `left` before `right`. The loops run over the operands of the relation whichever number is blank, so the order never depends on which number is shown. Pictures loop over the whole ascending, then the shown part ascending. Catalog: sentences addition, subtraction, or both, each within 5, 10 or 20 (`wholeMax` 5, 10 or `V1_NUMERIC_MAXIMUM`); pictures within 5, 10 or 20. Any other schema-valid `wholeMax` shows the Saved setting option (Appendix F). Crossing ten (D22, D-bonds-ten): a problem whose relation, whole = p + q, satisfies B.1's addition rule for its parts p and q (for an addition sentence its addends, for a subtraction sentence its subtrahend and result, for a picture its two parts) is left out under `regrouping` `without` and kept under `included`; that is exactly the carrying or borrowing its missing number needs, the canonical order applies to the filtered pool unchanged, and by default no problem makes or crosses ten.
